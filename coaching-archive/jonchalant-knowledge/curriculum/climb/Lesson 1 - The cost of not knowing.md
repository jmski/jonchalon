
**Headline:** *The cost of not {{knowing}}.*

**Core argument:**
You've been competent enough that the question of whether you're in the right work has stayed manageable. That manageability is itself the cost — it lets you postpone the question indefinitely. The Climb starts here because the inventory only works if you can feel what's at stake in *not* doing it.

**Why this lesson is here, in this position:**
Without this lesson, the next six feel like an academic exercise. Lesson 1 supplies motivational gravity. It also does the work of distinguishing this program from coaching-genre programs by refusing to promise a destination — the student leaves lesson 1 understanding the cost of not knowing, not a vision of what they'll know. The order matters: gravity before tools, not the reverse.

**Embodied component:**
Twice a day for five days — once in the morning before work, once in the evening after — sit still for two minutes and answer one question in writing: *Where in my body am I right now?* Use any vocabulary that comes (tight, light, heavy, blank, fizzing, locked). No interpretation. No goal of changing it. The instruction is to track, not to fix. By day five the student should have ten short entries that map onto the working day. The point is not the entries; the point is becoming someone who notices.

**Reflection prompt seed:**
*What has staying competent been costing you that you've stopped counting?*

(This question is meant to surface the things that have been re-framed as acceptable trade-offs — relationships deprioritized, hobbies dropped, body ignored. Not a list of complaints. A list of trades.)

**Voice notes:**
The temptation here is to motivate. Don't. Register A throughout. The lesson is not *"imagine what you could become"* — it's *"look honestly at what you've been quietly paying."* Banned-word risk: *unlock*, *transform*, *journey* will all want to appear in the framing. The opening sentence cannot promise an outcome. If the lesson reads like an invitation, rewrite it as a statement.
