## Summary

What began as a program restructure has evolved into a full brand build. The Foundation is no longer a single 8-week course built around four pillars; it's a three-stage / six-month coached engagement (**The Climb · The Vantage · The Leap**) sold alongside standalone modules, an Ongoing subscription for graduates, and (Year 2+) a Coach Training Program.

The brand has also evolved beyond "Jon teaches embodied presence." It's now **Jonchalant and the Iki-Guys** — a coaching practice + media brand built around the argument that embodiment transfers across mediums, demonstrated through a recurring podcast featuring guests whose mediums are different from Jon's.

Four structural decisions sit underneath everything in this document:

1. **The four-pillar framework is dissolved entirely.** Pillars do not survive as a teaching vocabulary, a card primitive, or a branded concept. The dancer/leader/writer triptych is retired. The kinetic line *"The medium changes — the fundamentals don't"* is retired with it.
2. **Embodiment is integrated deeply, not lightly.** In every lesson where it's possible, the body practice *is* the lesson — not an exercise appended to a concept. The concept is articulated after the body has felt it. This is the brand's competitive moat and cannot be compromised in service of faster content production.
3. **The visual frame is Silk Sonic, specifically.** Not "video game," not "hip hop/funk/R&B generally" — Silk Sonic late-night soul. Vintage warmth, intimate scale, restrained craft, swagger that earns itself. The aesthetic is *adjacent to* music, not pretending to be music. (See *Visual Frame* section.)
4. **The brand publicly includes the Iki-Guys from launch.** *Jonchalant and the Iki-Guys* is the visible brand name from day one. The Iki-Guys are featured guests and collaborators in a recurring podcast series — people whose work demonstrates the medium-agnostic embodiment claim. They are *not* trained coaches; that's a separate Year 2+ program with a different designation TBD.

## The three-stage structure

### Stage names (locked)

- **Stage 1 — The Climb**
- **Stage 2 — The Vantage**
- **Stage 3 — The Leap**

The names follow a climb–vantage–leap shape: effortful ascent to a viewpoint, an inhabited place from which you can finally see clearly, and the committed move from height into the change you've identified. Each name is body-anchored, parallel in form (definite article + monosyllable), and metaphorically coherent — the climb earns the vantage, the vantage prepares the leap, the leap completes the work the climb began.

*The Climb* is the inventory work. Effortful, deliberate, trust-based — you don't yet know what you'll see when you reach the top, but the work of getting there is the point.

*The Vantage* is the active integration work. Not a moment of seeing but a place you inhabit while you do the daily practice — designing rituals, building petals, having hard conversations. You can see more than you could on the ground; that visibility is what makes the Vantage work possible.

*The Leap* is the structural change — the load-bearing conversation, the move, the becoming. The metaphor is honest: you commit before you can see the landing. The Leap is exactly that.

**Origin note (internal only):** the naming arose from the Assassin's Creed synchronization sequence — climb the structure, survey the area, leap of faith. The reference is the spark, not the source. The climb-vantage-leap shape is universal (mountaineering traditions, Tibetan parables, hero's-journey scholarship, Tarot's Fool all have the same structure) and the brand inherits the structure without claiming any single tradition. **The AC reference must never appear in public copy.** It dates the brand and undercuts the universality.

**Stage names are not renamed under the band-frame pivot.** This was considered (The Demo · The Session · The Drop, The Riff · The Groove · The Break, etc.) and rejected. The climb-vantage-leap names earn their place because the work is genuinely shaped like that — they describe a *progression*, not a *vibe*. Musical alternatives sound cool but don't carry the meaning. The right place for band aesthetic is the *frame around* the work: visual identity, typography, photography, presentation. Not the names of the stages themselves. Substance underneath, swagger on top.

### What each stage is for

**Stage 1 — The Climb.** Honest cataloguing of what you already do, sorted into the four circles. The work is not discovery — it's inventory. The hard part is being honest about what doesn't fit and what falsely claims to fit. Time-bounded: a Climb buyer can complete the module's defined work in roughly 4-6 weeks of weekly practice.

**Stage 2 — The Vantage.** Finding where two circles overlap (outer petals) and building daily practice around the overlaps. Action-heavy. Time-bounded but longer than the Climb: most buyers complete the defined work in 12-16 weeks.

**Stage 3 — The Leap.** Growing the structurally missing circle. The work that's too big, too individual, or too long-horizon for a structured module. Coaching is structurally essential, not adjacent. Open-ended: 6-18 months of real work depending on the gap and the user's life context.

### Stage 1 lesson sketch (The Climb — 7 lessons)

1. **The cost of not knowing.** Body scan to surface what tenses up at "five more years of this."
2. **Honest self-assessment.** The three separations (love vs told-yourself-you-love; good-at vs well-compensated-for; world-needs vs assume-it-needs).
3. **The body as data source.** Embodiment as an inventory tool. Week of body-state tracking.
4. **Finding your physical medium.** Criteria for choosing one. Dance is Jon's; the lesson teaches the category.
5. **Cataloguing what you do.** The inventory itself. 20-minute moving practice before sorting.
6. **The circles you avoid.** Diagnosing the structurally missing circle. Speaking it aloud while moving.
7. **Mourning what isn't working.** Acceptance of rejection — by yourself, of yourself.

**Completion criteria:** populated inventory, named probable missing circle, chosen physical medium and started practicing it, named what's being let go of.

### Stage 2 lesson sketch (The Vantage — 10 lessons)

1. **Reading the overlaps you already have.** Map existing outer petals.
2. **Daily ritual design.** The mechanism for incorporating overlap into life.
3. **Communication as embodied skill — presence.** Active listening through the body.
4. **Communication as embodied skill — clarity.** Verbal/non-verbal alignment.
5. **Communication as embodied skill — assertiveness and tactical empathy.** Holding both at once.
6. **Emotional intelligence — patience and cultural competence.** Patience as body practice.
7. **Building relationships as a daily practice.** Three layers (personal, professional, casual).
8. **Saying no.** No while holding ground, not while retreating.
9. **Negotiating your current role.** Physical rehearsal in the medium.
10. **Self-management as embodied discipline.** Time, attention, growth mindset — as body practices.

**Completion criteria:** identified outer petals, at least one daily ritual held for four weeks, at least one hard conversation about role or relationships, at least one obligation dropped that wasn't growing a circle.

### Stage 3 lesson sketch (The Leap — 11 lessons; 8 effective per user)

1. **The missing circle, named in full.** Deeper diagnosis after the Vantage has produced real data.
2. **The cost of staying as you are.** Naming the structural feature in current life that's in the way.
3. **Mission as a missing circle.** *(Conditional)*
4. **Passion as a missing circle.** *(Conditional)*
5. **Vocation as a missing circle.** *(Conditional)*
6. **Profession as a missing circle.** *(Conditional)*
7. **The conversation.** The load-bearing conversation. Physical rehearsal. Coaching immediately before and after.
8. **Walking away.** Leaving as discipline, not failure.
9. **Confidence as evidence.** Late-program — confidence as byproduct of evidence accumulated.
10. **Trust and dependability as patterns.** 90-day commitment to visible reliability.
11. **Re-reading the ikigai.** Annual re-read as ongoing practice. Same body scan as Climb Lesson 1.

Lessons 3-6 are conditional — most users do one of the four depending on their gap. All four must exist as content.

**Completion criteria:** missing circle named in actionable language, load-bearing conversation completed, structural change made or consciously declined, visible evidence of new alignment, maintenance practice owned.

## Timeline truth

The Foundation is not an 8-week program. The current copy promise *"Eight weeks. One week at a time"* is retired. The truth, which is also the marketing message:

> The Climb takes 4-6 weeks. The Vantage takes 12-16 weeks. The Leap takes 6-18 months. **The Foundation is a 6-month coached engagement** that gets users through the Climb and the Vantage and into the Leap. Leap work continues beyond the formal engagement, supported by the Ongoing subscription for those who want it.

*"As long as it takes"* is the brand voice expression of this. Competitors sell speed; Jonchalant sells honesty about pace.

| **Module 2 — The Vantage** (standalone) | $750 | 10 lessons, lifetime access, AI tools (3 uses/month) |
| **Modules 1 + 2 bundle** | $1,100 | Both modules, $150 saving |
| **The Foundation** | $5,000 list · $4,500 founding cohort | All 3 modules (Climb + Vantage + Leap) + 16 1:1 sessions + monthly group call + weekly check-ins + unlimited AI tools, 6-month engagement |
| **Top-up sessions** | $350 each · 4-pack $1,200 | Active Foundation clients only |
| **Ongoing subscription** | $300/month | Foundation graduates only. 1 session/month + monthly group call + AI tools. Capped at 40 subscribers. |
| **Coach Training Program** | $12,000 | *Year 2 launch.* Foundation graduates only. 12-18 month engagement. Trainees are *not* called Iki-Guys; that designation is reserved for podcast guests. Working title TBD — possibly *Jonchalant Practitioners* or similar. |

### What's not purchasable

- **The Leap standalone is not offered.** Available only inside the Foundation.
- **No payment plans at launch.** May revisit if a meaningful number of qualified buyers are constrained by lump sum.

### Pricing principles

- **Standalone modules price-anchor low and exist as funnel.** Not the margin engine. Credit toward Foundation if upgraded.
- **Foundation is the engine.** Premium pricing forced by the capacity model.
- **Ongoing is the stability layer.** Low coaching time, alumni retention. Target: 50% of graduates convert over 2-3 years.
- **Top-ups priced higher per-session than bundled.** The bundle must visibly be the better deal.
- **Coach Training priced as serious investment, not aspirational lottery ticket.**

### Upgrade credit policy

Standalone module purchase price credits forward in full toward the Foundation. Protects the funnel — buyers don't feel punished for trying the entry product first.

### Founding cohort pricing — brand voice constraint

Framed as fact, not as urgency tactic: *"The Foundation runs in cohorts of 4-6. Founding-cohort pricing — $4,500 — applies to the first cohort. After that, $5,000."* No marketing-genre language. Voice integrity over conversion rate.

The $5,000 list price must be what Jon would actually charge a random buyer without scarcity pressure. If Jon would take $4,500 from anyone, then $5,000 is fictional anchoring.

## Visual frame — Silk Sonic specifically

The brand visual frame is Silk Sonic late-night soul, *specifically* — not "video game," not "hip hop/funk/R&B generally," not "Bruno Mars solo pop." The distinction matters because each direction has a different visual language.

### Why Silk Sonic specifically

- **Referential and warm.** Honoring a tradition (vintage soul) while making it new. Mirrors what Jonchalant does with ikigai.
- **Intimate scale.** Two people, not a stadium act. A small operation done with exceptional craft.
- **Highly crafted.** Every detail intentional. Matches the brand voice already.
- **Playful without being cheap.** Swagger that earns itself, not bravado.
- **Distinctive without being loud.** Velvet, gold, low light, smoke, vintage microphones — not neon and chrome.
- **The music for late at night, when you're being honest with yourself.** Which is exactly the mood the Foundation work happens in.

### What this means visually

**Palette direction (to be tested against existing tokens):** vintage gold, deep velvet plum, smoke gray, ivory cream, oxblood, brass. The existing burnt indigo / muted moss / warm amber palette may partially survive — the warm amber could become brass; the moss probably retires. Palette decisions belong in a dedicated mood-board session before being locked.

**Typography direction:** Fraunces likely survives because of its vintage Garamond/Caslon spirit. May want a display face with more theatrical personality alongside Fraunces for headline moments. To be tested.

**Photography direction:** retires mountain/vista/landscape. New direction is *low light, warm interiors, vintage microphones (literally — could be a real prop on the About page), velvet textures, single-spotlight portraits, late-night studio energy, hands on instruments, body in motion mid-phrase*. Jon photographed in this aesthetic specifically — not in a dance studio with overhead fluorescents, but in low warm light.

**Iconography / visual moments:** the three stage cards may take *album-side / track listing* treatments. Each lesson could be styled as a track. The Foundation is the album; Ongoing is the b-sides and rarities. Tasteful metaphor extension, not forced.

### Risks to manage

- **Costume problem.** Funk/soul aesthetics on a non-musician can read as performative. The aesthetic must be *adjacent to* the music (Jon's medium is dance, which is musical), not pretending to *be* the music. No fake stage poses, no album covers Jon couldn't credibly star in.
- **Corporate audience.** The target buyer is well-paid corporate professionals in their 30s-40s. They'll respond to a confident, distinctive aesthetic but recoil from stage-show energy. "Warm and honest" stays the dominant brand quality; Silk Sonic frames it.
- **Multi-aesthetic confusion.** *Silk Sonic specifically* is the lock. Don't drift toward Bruno Mars pop or hip-hop visuals during execution.

### Open visual decisions

- Full palette lock (mood-board session required)
- Typography pairing (Fraunces + display face TBD)
- Logo design (commission a Toronto-area brand designer; ~$1,500-$4,000; not Midjourney work)
- Photography commission (12-18 months out, real photographer for hero imagery)
- Icon/mark design (likely abstract three-element mark; not literal mountain)

## The session pool model

The Foundation uses a session pool rather than fixed coaching cadence. 16 sessions over six months, bookable when the client needs them.

### Why pool, not cadence

Brand voice — *"intentional, also at our own pace"* — argues against fixed weekly cadence. Some clients need most support during the Climb; others save sessions for the Leap. Forcing one cadence serves the coach, not the client.

### Pacing rules (built into the program)

- **Maximum 4 sessions per month** (prevents front-loading collapse)
- **Maximum 90 days between sessions during active engagement** (prevents disappearing)
- **At least 2 sessions reserved for Leap work** unless the client explicitly opts out

### Anti-hoarding nudges

Scheduled session prompts in curriculum at high-leverage moments. Quarterly async check-ins showing session balance. Mid-engagement re-read at 3 months (scheduled session disguised as milestone). Group call effect — hearing other members talk about recent sessions normalizes use.

## The Iki-Guys (featured guests + podcast series)

### Definition (locked)

**An Iki-Guy is someone whose work and life align — *visibly*, *honestly*, *across decades* — and who can talk about the alignment without bullshit.**

Three load-bearing words:

- **Visibly.** Alignment shows up in how they live, not how they post about it. Proof is on the ground.
- **Honestly.** They can talk about the work without sanitizing the cost. They mourned what they had to mourn. They don't pretend the path was clean.
- **Across decades.** A 26-year-old who "found their calling" two years ago is not an Iki-Guy. Time tested the alignment.

The "helps others" filter: **they teach what they know, or share it generously, in their medium.** Workshop runners, mentors, public-facing practitioners. Pure-practitioner-who-doesn't-teach is excluded because the whole point of an Iki-Guy interview is the *conversation about the work* — they need to be able to articulate the path, not just be on it.

### Why Iki-Guys are featured guests, not trained coaches

The Iki-Guys name was originally considered as the long-term scaling name for trained coaches under the Coach Training Program. That interpretation was rejected. Instead:

- **Iki-Guys = featured guests in the podcast series.** Launchable from day one. Demonstrates the medium-agnostic embodiment claim with real humans.
- **Trained coaches (Year 2+) get a different designation.** Workshopped when the Coach Training Program is real. Possibly *Jonchalant Practitioners* or similar. The Iki-Guys name is *not* available for that program.
- **Overlap allowed but rare.** A future trained coach who happens to also be a featured Iki-Guy (because they're a decade-deep practitioner in their original medium) gets both designations. The rare overlap is meaningful, not the default.

### What the Iki-Guys series does for the brand

- **Proves the medium-agnostic claim.** A fitness pro, a photographer, a chef, a craftsperson — each demonstrating their version of alignment makes the dancer/leader/writer claim concrete with real humans.
- **Builds an audio library that becomes a moat.** Dozens of hours of voices in different mediums describing the same underlying alignment work. Quotable, citable, referenceable.
- **Markets the Foundation without feeling like marketing.** A listener experiences Jon's interviewing voice for 60 minutes — that's a sample of his coaching presence, made public.
- **Solves the brand-naming question elegantly.** *Jonchalant and the Iki-Guys* is band-naming syntax that works because there IS a band: Jon + recurring guests.

### Candidate vetting

Don't apply the triad as a gatekeeping test to incomplete profiles. Use the triad as a self-check before reaching out:
- Is their alignment visible in body of work, not just presentation?
- Can they speak honestly about the cost, the mourning, the scars?
- Have they been in their medium long enough for it to be tested?
- Do they teach/share/mentor in some form?

## The podcast (Iki-Guys series format)

### Locked format

- **Audio-first.** Conversation recorded in good quality. 45-75 minutes per episode.
- **Long-form.** No chopping into 10-minute clips. The conversation is the product.
- **Lightly produced.** Two people, two microphones, real conversation. Cold open from the most striking moment, then back to the start. 5-second motif at most, no overproduced intro music.
- **Companion blog post per episode.** Not a transcript dump — a short framing: who the guest is, why they're an Iki-Guy, three quotes, link to listen.
- **Video optional, decided per episode.** Recorded alongside audio when convenient; not the default deliverable.

### Why podcast format fits

- **Conversational depth is the differentiator.** Most fitness/photography podcasts ask "your three tips for X." Jonchalant asks "what did you give up to stay in it" and "what does your body know that took you a decade to articulate."
- **Audio scales operationally.** Single edit pass per episode is feasible for a one-person operation.
- **Long-form fits the voice.** Brand is "warm and honest, not polished and aspirational." Long-form conversation is the most on-voice content format.
- **Marketing without feeling like marketing.** Hearing Jon as an interviewer IS marketing for the Foundation.

### Production setup (Phase 11/12)

- Microphone: Shure SM7B ($400) or Rode PodMic ($130) — both fine
- Audio interface (if XLR): Focusrite Scarlett ($170)
- Editing: Descript ($20/mo — transcription + editing + filler word removal in one)
- Hosting: Transistor, Buzzsprout, or Spotify for Podcasters

All-in cost: $600-800 equipment + $20/mo software.

### Production cadence

**One Iki-Guy episode every 4-6 weeks.** This is a real commitment, not "when we have time." Series die from inconsistent cadence. Prep is real (3-5 hours of research per episode for the questions that haven't been asked before). Scheduling + recording + editing + companion blog post is probably 8-12 hours of work per episode. **Build this into Jon's weekly hours from launch.**

### Launch-day content state

**Site goes live with 3 Iki-Guy podcast episodes already in the feed.** First-time visitor on launch day sees an established podcast with a back catalog, not "Episode 1 coming soon." This means: **record 2-3 episodes pre-launch, queued for release alongside the site.** Operational priority once recording setup is ready.

## The coach training pathway (Year 2+)

Scaling does not happen by Jon taking more clients. It happens by Foundation graduates becoming coaches who take their own clients under the Jonchalant umbrella. *Not* called Iki-Guys; that name is reserved for podcast guests. Working title for the program: *Coach Training Program*. Working title for graduates: TBD (possibly *Jonchalant Practitioners*).

### Why this fits the brand

The strongest coaches are people who've done the work themselves. For Foundation graduates with a Mission gap specifically, coaching in the methodology that helped them is a genuinely viable Mission — an honest answer to a real problem, not a revenue trick.

### Long-term timeline

- **Year 1:** Foundation launches. Coach Training Program mapped conceptually.
- **Year 2:** Coach Training Program built. Top 1-2 Year-1 graduates invited.
- **Year 3:** First trained coach begins taking clients. Revenue-share arrangement TBD.
- **Year 4-5:** 3-5 trained coaches active. Jon's primary work shifts to training/supervising coaches, brand & content, Ongoing subscribers, select Foundation clients.

### Brand designation when team scales

*Jonchalant* stays the parent brand and method. Trained coaches operate under it. Framing: *"the Jonchalant method, taught by Jon-trained coaches."* Standard founder-named-with-licensed-method structure (cf. Marshall Goldsmith, Brené Brown's Daring Way).
