# Program Structure Strategy

> Source-of-truth for the Jonchalant program re-architecture. Drafted May 2026.
> This document is the output of a strategy session and the input to Workstream 2 (Sanity schema redesign) and the canonical copy rewrite that follows.

---

## Summary

The Foundation is being restructured from a single 8-week course built around the four pillars (Grounding · Energy · Flow · Command) into a three-stage program built around the ikigai progression: **identify the circles → find the overlaps → integrate the missing circle.**

Two structural decisions sit underneath every choice in this document:

1. **The four-pillar framework is dissolved entirely.** Pillars do not survive as a teaching vocabulary, a card primitive, or a branded concept. The dancer/leader/writer triptych is retired. The kinetic line *"The medium changes — the fundamentals don't"* is retired with it.
2. **Embodiment is integrated deeply, not lightly.** In every lesson where it's possible, the body practice *is* the lesson — not an exercise appended to a concept. The concept is articulated after the body has felt it. This is the brand's competitive moat and cannot be compromised in service of faster content production.

These two decisions together complete the repositioning that began with "find your ikigai and learn to inhabit it." The pillars were a remnant of the prior "executive presence taught through dance" framing. Removing them finishes the move.

## The three-stage structure

### Working names

- **Stage 1 — Inventory**
- **Stage 2 — Petals**
- **Stage 3 — Integration**

Only *Petals* feels like brand voice. *Inventory* and *Integration* are placeholders pending a naming pass. Final names should be verb-energy (the brand promises *doing* something embodied, not understanding something cognitive) and parallel in structure. Naming is a separate task.

### What each stage is for

**Stage 1 — Inventory.**
Honest cataloguing of what you already do, sorted into the four circles. The work is not discovery — it's inventory. The hard part is being honest about what doesn't fit and what falsely claims to fit. Time-bounded: a Stage 1 buyer can complete the module's defined work in roughly 4-6 weeks of weekly practice.

**Stage 2 — Petals.**
Finding where two circles overlap (outer petals) and building daily practice around the overlaps. Action-heavy. Time-bounded but longer than Stage 1: most buyers complete the defined work in 12-16 weeks. The "outer petals" terminology has light precedent in the wider ikigai conversation (one Times Higher Education article from 2024 uses similar language) but the metaphor is clarifying enough to keep.

**Stage 3 — Integration.**
Growing the structurally missing circle. The work that's too big, too individual, or too long-horizon for a structured module. Coaching is structurally essential, not adjacent. Open-ended: 6-18 months of real work depending on the gap and the user's life context.

### Stage 1 lesson sketch (7 lessons)

1. **The cost of not knowing.** Naming what continued misalignment costs. Body scan to surface what tenses up at the thought of "five more years of this."
2. **Honest self-assessment.** The three separations (love vs told-yourself-you-love; good-at vs well-compensated-for; world-needs vs assume-it-needs). Body opens/contracts pre-cognitively.
3. **The body as data source.** Embodiment introduced as an inventory tool, not just a practice. Week of body-state tracking.
4. **Finding your physical medium.** Criteria for choosing one: requires presence, offers feedback, sustainable for years, not status-driven. Dance is Jon's; the lesson teaches the category.
5. **Cataloguing what you do.** The inventory itself. Sort everything into circles (or none). 20-minute moving practice before sorting.
6. **The circles you avoid.** Diagnosing the structurally missing circle and the story you tell yourself about the gap. Speaking it aloud while moving.
7. **Mourning what isn't working.** Acceptance of rejection — by yourself, of yourself. Mourning as discipline.

**Completion criteria:** populated inventory, named probable missing circle, chosen physical medium and started practicing it, named what's being let go of.

### Stage 2 lesson sketch (10 lessons)

1. **Reading the overlaps you already have.** Map existing outer petals.
2. **Daily ritual design.** The mechanism for incorporating overlap into life. Designed and tested physical ritual.
3. **Communication as embodied skill — part 1: presence.** Active listening through the body.
4. **Communication as embodied skill — part 2: clarity.** Verbal/non-verbal alignment. Body either supports the message or contradicts it.
5. **Communication as embodied skill — part 3: assertiveness and tactical empathy.** Holding both at once.
6. **Emotional intelligence — patience and cultural competence.** Patience as a body practice. Working against physical defaults.
7. **Building relationships as a daily practice.** Three layers (personal, professional, casual). Compounding small investments.
8. **Saying no.** The subtraction counterweight. No while holding ground, not while retreating.
9. **Negotiating your current role.** The conversation with the manager. Physical rehearsal in the medium.
10. **Self-management as embodied discipline.** Time, attention, growth mindset — as body practices.

**Completion criteria:** identified outer petals, at least one daily ritual held for four weeks, at least one hard conversation about role or relationships, at least one obligation dropped that wasn't growing a circle.

### Stage 3 lesson sketch (11 lessons; 8 effective per user)

1. **The missing circle, named in full.** Deeper diagnosis after Stage 2 has produced real data.
2. **The cost of staying as you are.** Naming the structural feature in current life that's in the way.
3. **Mission as a missing circle.** *(Conditional)* The work of building a Mission you own rather than borrow.
4. **Passion as a missing circle.** *(Conditional)* Sustained engagement, not searching. 12-week deliberate practice commitment.
5. **Vocation as a missing circle.** *(Conditional)* Pricing your work honestly. Market test.
6. **Profession as a missing circle.** *(Conditional)* Building competence, not credentials.
7. **The conversation.** The load-bearing conversation each user has to have. Physical rehearsal. Coaching immediately before and after.
8. **Walking away.** Leaving as discipline, not failure. Distinguishing walking from running. Multi-session coaching.
9. **Confidence as evidence.** Late-program — confidence as byproduct of evidence accumulated.
10. **Trust and dependability as patterns.** 90-day commitment to visible reliability.
11. **Re-reading the ikigai.** Annual re-read as ongoing practice. Same body scan as Stage 1 Lesson 1 — note what's changed.

Lessons 3-6 are conditional — most users do one of the four depending on their gap. All four must exist as content, even though most users only consume one.

**Completion criteria:** missing circle named in actionable language, load-bearing conversation completed, structural change made or consciously declined, visible evidence of new alignment, maintenance practice owned.

## Timeline truth

The Foundation is not an 8-week program. The current copy promise "Eight weeks. One week at a time" implies that finishing the program produces ikigai. This is not true and selling it as such would erode the brand voice.

The truth, which is also the marketing message:

> Stage 1 takes 4-6 weeks. Stage 2 takes 12-16 weeks. Stage 3 takes 6-18 months. **The Foundation is a 6-month coached engagement** that gets users through Stage 1 and 2 and into Stage 3. Stage 3 work continues beyond the formal engagement, supported by the Ongoing subscription for those who want it.

"As long as it takes" is the brand voice expression of this. It's directly Register A and it's the most defensible position any program in this space can take: competitors sell speed, Jonchalant sells honesty about pace.

## The product structure

### What's purchasable

| Product | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|
| **Module 1 — Inventory** (standalone) | $500 | 7 lessons, lifetime access, AI tools (3 uses/month) |
| **Module 2 — Petals** (standalone) | $750 | 10 lessons, lifetime access, AI tools (3 uses/month) |
| **Modules 1 + 2 bundle** | $1,100 | Both modules, $150 saving |
| **The Foundation** | $5,000 list · $4,500 founding cohort | All 3 modules + 16 1:1 sessions + monthly group call + weekly check-ins + unlimited AI tools, 6-month engagement |
| **Top-up sessions** | $350 each · 4-pack $1,200 | Active Foundation clients only |
| **Ongoing subscription** | $300/month | Foundation graduates only. 1 session/month + monthly group call + AI tools. Capped at 40 subscribers. |
| **Coach Training Program** | $12,000 | *Year 2 launch.* Foundation graduates only. 12-18 month engagement. |

### What's not purchasable

- **Stage 3 standalone is not offered.** Stage 3 work requires deep coaching and full prerequisite work. Available only inside the Foundation.
- **No payment-plan tier** is being introduced at launch. May revisit later if a meaningful number of qualified buyers are constrained by lump sum.

### Pricing principles

- **Standalone modules price-anchor low and exist as funnel.** They're not the margin engine. They prove the product, qualify the buyer, and credit toward Foundation if upgraded.
- **Foundation is the engine.** Premium pricing is forced by the capacity model — 5-10 active clients × 2 cohorts/year × price = revenue ceiling. Below $4,000, the business doesn't function.
- **Ongoing subscription is the stability layer.** Light coaching time, high perceived value, alumni retention. Conversion target: 50% of graduates over 2-3 years.
- **Top-up sessions are priced higher per-session than bundled** ($350 vs ~$280 inside Foundation). The bundle must visibly be the better deal.
- **Coach Training is priced as serious investment, not aspirational lottery ticket.** A graduate who actually coaches recoups the cost through real client work in 12-18 months. Pricing is honest, not predatory.

### Upgrade credit policy

A buyer who has purchased standalone Module 1, Module 2, or the bundle gets the full purchase price credited toward the Foundation if they upgrade. This protects the funnel — buyers don't feel punished for trying the entry product first.

### Founding cohort pricing — brand voice constraint

The early-bird mechanic must be framed in voice. **No "limited time offer," "save $500 today," or marketing-genre urgency language.** The voice-correct framing is something like:

> *The Foundation runs in cohorts of 4-6. Founding-cohort pricing — $4,500 — applies to the first cohort. After that, $5,000.*

This is honest (the price genuinely goes up), creates real urgency (the cohort genuinely fills), and doesn't slide into the dark-pattern coaching-industry tactics the brand explicitly positions against.

The $5,000 list price must be the price Jon would actually charge a random Tuesday buyer without scarcity pressure. If Jon would take $4,500 from a random buyer, then $5,000 is fictional anchoring and the founding cohort framing is dishonest. Voice integrity over conversion rate.

## Capacity model

### Jon's capacity ceiling

**5-10 active Foundation clients at any time.** Most weeks 8-12 hours of coaching contact. This is a deliberate choice — Jon is maintaining part-time work at Life Time, family responsibilities, and his own embodiment practice. Capacity exceeds this in fits and starts but not as a sustainable baseline.

### Why this matters for pricing

At 8 simultaneous clients × 2 cohorts/year = 16 Foundation clients annually. At $4,750 average price (mix of founding and list), that's $76K from Foundation. The model only works because:

- The Ongoing subscription compounds across years as cohorts graduate
- Standalone modules require effectively zero Jon time per sale
- The Coach Training Program scales horizontally in Year 3+ when graduates begin taking clients

### Revenue floor (Year 2-3 steady state)

| Line | Revenue |
|---|---|
| Foundation | ~$76,000 |
| Ongoing (30 subscribers) | $108,000 |
| Standalone modules | $32,500 |
| Top-ups (~30% take-up) | $6,000 |
| Coach Training (3 trainees/year, Year 2+) | $36,000 |
| **Total** | **~$258,500** |

Strong execution: $300-350K. The Ongoing subscription is the most strategically important line — nearly 40% of revenue at this stage, lowest hours-per-dollar.

## The session pool model

The Foundation uses a session pool rather than a fixed coaching cadence. 16 sessions over six months, bookable when the client needs them.

### Why pool, not cadence

The brand voice — *"while we're being intentional, we're also doing it at our own pace"* — argues against a fixed weekly cadence. Some clients need most of their support in Stage 1 (the inventory and mourning work is hardest for them). Others breeze through Stage 1 and need all their sessions in Stage 3. Forcing one cadence on everyone serves the coach, not the client.

### Pacing rules

The pool model has two failure modes that need structural prevention:

1. **Clients hoard.** Save sessions for a "real problem" that never comes. Finish the program having used half their pool.
2. **Clients dump everything early.** Use all sessions in the first two months, then disappear when Stage 3 work begins — exactly when coaching is most essential.

Both are prevented by rules built into the program:

- **Maximum 4 sessions per month** (prevents front-loading collapse)
- **Maximum 90 days between sessions during active engagement** (prevents disappearing)
- **At least 2 sessions reserved for Stage 3 work**, unless the client explicitly opts out in writing (forces awareness)

### Anti-hoarding nudges

- **Scheduled session prompts in the curriculum.** Module 1 Lesson 1 ends with "Book your first session before starting Lesson 2." Similar prompts at high-leverage moments throughout (after Lesson 6 "circles you avoid," after Lesson 7 "mourning," before "the conversation," before "walking away").
- **Quarterly check-ins from Jon.** Async note showing session balance and asking where they are in the work.
- **Mid-engagement re-read at 3 months.** Scheduled session specifically for inventory review — milestone framing, but functions as a use-your-sessions reminder.
- **Group call effect.** Hearing other members talk about their recent sessions normalizes session use.

## The coach training pathway (Year 2+)

This is the most strategically important long-term decision in the business. Scaling does not happen by Jon taking more clients. It happens by Foundation graduates becoming coaches who take their own clients under the Jonchalant umbrella.

### Why this fits the brand

The strongest coaches in any modality are the people who've done the work themselves. A Foundation graduate who went through Stage 3 work, found their missing circle, integrated it, and built a life around it is exactly the kind of person who has authority to coach someone else through the same work.

For corporate professionals with a Mission gap specifically — the most common gap in the target audience — becoming a coach in the methodology that helped them find their own answer is a genuinely viable Mission. **This is the structural insight that justifies the program existing:** it's an honest answer to a real problem for a specific subset of graduates, not a revenue trick.

Most coaching certifications are priced cynically — high because the program is the product, and graduates are sold the dream of recouping the cost. The Jonchalant coach training is priced honestly: a graduate who actually coaches recoups the cost through real client work in 12-18 months. The promise is real, not aspirational.

### Long-term timeline

- **Year 1:** Foundation launches. Coach Training Program mapped conceptually but not built.
- **Year 2:** Coach Training Program built. Top 1-2 Year-1 graduates (Mission-gap profile, deep engagement) invited.
- **Year 3:** First trained coach begins taking clients under Jonchalant umbrella. Revenue-share arrangement (exact split TBD).
- **Year 4-5:** 3-5 trained coaches active. Jon's primary work shifts to: training/supervising coaches, brand and content, Ongoing subscribers, select Foundation clients.

### Implications for current decisions

- **Foundation must produce coach-grade graduates by design.** Not every graduate. Enough that the pipeline produces 1-2 candidates per year by Year 2.
- **Stage 3 work in particular must produce articulable practice**, not just personal change. A graduate who can't articulate how they integrated their missing circle can't coach someone else through the same work.
- **The pathway should be visible at enrollment**, not as a hook (most won't want it) but for the 5-15% of graduates who will. Plants the seed early.
- **Brand naming.** "Jonchalant" is Jon-centric. Once other coaches enter the picture, this needs thought — likely positioning as *"the Jonchalant method, taught by Jon-trained coaches"* with Jon remaining the central voice and framework owner.

## Brand voice non-negotiables for this work

Lifting these out so they don't get lost in implementation prompts:

- **No coaching-genre language.** Banned words still apply: unlock, transform, journey, empowered, authentic self, limiting beliefs, inner game. Add for this rebuild: *transformation*, *breakthrough*, *next level*.
- **No urgency tactics.** Founding cohort pricing is the only urgency mechanic, and it's framed as fact, not as offer.
- **"As long as it takes" is the timing message everywhere.** Not "in just 6 months" — "the Foundation is a 6-month coached engagement, and the work continues."
- **Premium pricing is presented honestly.** Show the list price. Show what's included. Don't apologize for it. Don't bury it.
- **Embodiment is the moat.** Every lesson description, every page section, every marketing surface should make it clear that the body practice is the lesson — not an add-on to a concept.
